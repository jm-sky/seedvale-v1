## 1. Incident identity: same problem versus a similar problem

1. **World reality is authoritative.** Give each consequential subject a stable identity (particular animal, den, NPC, structure, container, site). Give each **concrete occurrence** of a problem a durable `incidentId`, distinct from species, problem category, neighbourhood, quest name/definition or quest offer.
2. **Do not merge by similarity.** Two livestock losses may be concurrent and entirely legitimate: two different animals, owners, places or causes. The **same animal can be lost, recovered, then lost again**; each distinct event gets a new occurrence ID. Do not erase the first resolved incident or reuse its ID.
3. **Do not multiply one occurrence.** A single physical absence/theft of a specific animal is one incident even if an authored quest, a notice-board job and an NPC conversation all describe it. They may be separate *presentations or compatible objectives* of that incident, but may not manufacture a second missing animal, independent contradictory custody, or mutually exclusive rescue claims. Do not infer identity merely from a matching animal ID if the event happened at a different time; compare the **actual loss/theft occurrence**.
4. **Authored and generated opportunities coexist.** A real, unrelated stray, wolf den, treasure find or pest outbreak can generate its own opportunity near authored content. Authoring reserves only a **specific physical incident/asset** and any exclusive mutation claims, not all nearby opportunities of the same category.
5. **Repeatability is real, not fabricated.** Resolved incidents remain persistently resolved (or retain a durable tombstone); an actual recurrence uses a fresh ID and real world transition. No quest activation resets a cleared den, invents a replacement animal, or reopens a recovered loss.

Examples: Molly Lambert's stolen lamb **Pip** in G01 is the same specific animal/theft throughout that incident. A different household's missing lamb is a different incident. If Pip is recovered and strays later, that new event is a new incident, not a reactivation of the theft. V1 `zagubiona-owca` is a generated mechanism, not an always-unique authored instance ([D-QUEST-9/11](lost-livestock-quest-curation.md), [D-QUEST-42](g01-lost-lamb-and-quest-traveller-protection-curation.md)). Likewise Greybeard, the short V1 dangerous-wolf/den thread and the settlement pack remain different authored subjects/incidents; an actual shared pack/den in `wilki-pod-osada` and merged `wilki-u-kupca` is **one** incident ([wolf curation](wolf-quest-curation.md)).

## 2. Claim, objective and reward policy

- Each world transition has a replay-stable **world-event identity**, timestamp/order and actual subjects; the owning world subsystem applies the physical outcome **once**. The quest system reads facts and does not own a competing copy of a dead wolf, recovered sheep, repaired roof or inventory transfer.
- Admission checks **specific incident identity** and incompatible **exclusive physical claims** (e.g. killing that same named animal or clearing that same den). Non-exclusive observations, helping, reporting and compatible objectives can coexist. First accepted commitments are reconciled rather than silently revoked when a later authored presentation becomes eligible. Authoring precedence concerns **narrative framing**, not a global ban on generated jobs.
- Store quest *definition* separately from quest *instance*; objective achieved, knowledge/acceptance, reporting and closure are different facts. Actual NPC actions or player actions before quest acceptance can resolve the world. Credit and outcome must follow **real participation/evidence and authored criteria**, not mere proximity or quest activation. Independently completed NPC work can close or change an offer **only for stories marked eligible for NPC resolution** ([D-QUEST-46](DECISIONS.md#author-clarifications--2026-10-19), author 2026-10-19: selective, decided per quest; never a blanket rule that uses up the player's adventures).
- **Different legitimate reward entitlements may coexist** (AD-42): one animal rescue, wolf death or den clearing may satisfy two compatible objectives and justify two independently sourced rewards. This is **not** permission to pay the **same entitlement twice**. Apply effects and each reward slot with persisted idempotency receipts; use actual payer accounts, goods, money, ownership and relationship state (no minted quest-only stock).
- Use deterministic tie-breaking when two actors/interventions coincide. The final physical resolution is exactly one event; quest journals/observers may reference it more than once without duplicating it.

This is the accepted resolution of the previously open **D-QUEST-16 / `world:wolf-den-pressure`** question. The proposed `incidentId`, `worldEventId`, claim-set and `effectKey` names from Review 095 are illustrative and **reversible technical choices** pending Opus sign-off and M7 detailed design.

## 3. Persistent identity, property and access

- Selectively preserve **global identity** for meaningful individual items (e.g. the elder's unique sword, named heirloom, evidence, a particular stolen tool) and **lot identity** where a fungible stock has distinct title/provenance (e.g. mixed-owner shipwreck cargo). Ordinary homogeneous commodity items retain the existing efficient bag/stack model; do not assign expensive global IDs to everything.
- Keep **physical custody**, **legal title**, claimant, source/provenance and **permission to act/access** separate. Possession alone is not proof of legal ownership; a player may hold stolen goods without the world instantly knowing of theft. Witnesses, manifests and NPC knowledge are evidence; no omniscient guilt. Grave inspection permits a named target and scope, not every cemetery.
- Reuse the current V3 `Inventory`, `Account`, trade, world overlays, and existing ownership/knowledge authorities. Do not introduce quest-only inventories, legal/crime engine or property database.
- **Verified V1 precedents, not automatically accepted V3 code:** `docs/comparison/code/seedvale-v1/src/fauna/animalOwnership.ts` has one discriminated `AnimalOwner` for household/player; `src/settlement/landOwnership.ts` saves sparse `settlementId:plotId` keys; `src/items/foreignProperty.ts` treats unauthorised use of a merchant's horse as foreign property; `src/items/inventoryTransfer.ts` models reversible inventory transfer checks. V1 plans `items-player-052` and `items-player-053` discuss stable owned village tools and lossless pickup, but a plan is not proof every behaviour shipped. **Adapt the small proven ideas**; V3 currently has inventory-local item IDs and needs a selective, interoperable design.

## 4. Save/load, simulation ownership and staged scope

- Use explicit **versioned, validated save sections** for incidents, meaningful assets/lots, ownership/custody, quest progress, durable world changes, evidence/knowledge where appropriate, RNG continuations and applied-effect receipts. Commit related world, stock, payment and quest changes as one logical transition; load/retry must not duplicate effects. Fit [Save V1 decisions](save-v1-format.md); do not invent a generic `questFlags` bag.
- Near/Mid agent state and actions share the same actor/incident identities. Resolve chronological interventions deterministically, irrespective of reduced tick frequency. **Far is intentionally not autonomous before M8**: do not simulate nonexistent off-screen wolf kills, rescue, production, death, theft or settlement repairs to satisfy an M7 quest deadline. At M8, hand off aggregate/individual state and unfinished commitments exactly once.
- Bind concrete quest people, animals, sites, caches, source items and mutually exclusive claims to the **actual authored canonical world** during M3/M6/M7 placement, not by animal kind, distance or reference-catalogue labels. Unsupported economy/companions/transport/crime/building/mine-colony branches remain staged to M8/M9/M10+; preserve the stories without special-case substitute engines.
- Allow a small real item/coin exchange backed by shared inventory/trade before a regional economy exists; no imaginary supply, instant inter-settlement fulfilment or quest currency. D-NPC-22 already governs NPC incidental location discovery; do not reopen it or invent NPC exploration AI.

## 5. Predator finishing: author-confirmed shared-health boundary

Author confirmed on 2026-10-14 that a predator **does not automatically kill an NPC at 0 HP**. The NPC falls incapacitated; **the animal must carry out a separate finishing strike** to kill by predation. In ordinary unthreatened conditions, the animal has **roughly a 50% baseline chance to abandon finishing**; greater hunger reduces that chance. **Self-defence takes priority:** if another NPC or animal actively threatens the predator during the finishing attempt, it switches to defending itself or fleeing, interrupting the finishing strike rather than executing through incoming attacks. The author accepted the recommended **option A** for threat behaviour, which increases abandonment of the immediate finishing attempt; future pursuit or a new attack must arise from ordinary animal AI, not an uninterruptible pending execution. Curve, decision sampling, downed duration, attack timing and reaction to new threats remain **M6 tuning/verification**, using deterministic RNG and shared state, not one custom probability per quest.

This is different from medical, environmental and illness death, which may happen through shared NPC health without a predator's finishing strike. Human-on-human combat remains nonlethal at 0 HP without a later deliberate finishing blow. The player still has the forgiving knock-out path ([D-VISION-12](DECISIONS.md)).

## 6. Implementation gates and test obligations (not author choices)

**Architecture gate:** Opus/architecture owner checks the proposed identifiers and transactional/event owner boundary against existing `D-ARCH-46`, M3/M4/M5/M6, save and economic code before a detailed M7 plan is signed off. Acceptance of this contract closes **product-policy arbitration**, not the absence of a runtime engine.

**Required deterministic scenarios:** (1) two different lost animals at once; (2) the same recovered animal genuinely lost twice; (3) two quest perspectives on **one** theft/loss; (4) Pip recovered before offer; (5) distinct wolf dens and one shared den; (6) one event credited to multiple compatible objectives with distinct allowed payouts; (7) repeated event delivery, report, payout and save/load are idempotent; (8) traded/stolen unique item retains identity, provenance and title; (9) shipwreck lots of mixed ownership never become an undifferentiated reward; (10) named grave permission does not generalise; (11) NPC autonomous resolution cannot race player resolution; (12) mid-to-near promotion and M8 Far reification neither invent nor double-resolve actions; (13) predator downs NPC without killing, abandons about half the time at the calibrated neutral baseline, reacts to hunger, and **interrupts finishing under threat**; (14) medical/non-combat death remains a separate shared-health path. No simulation tests are claimed run by this document.
