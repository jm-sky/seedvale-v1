## 1. Executive summary

V1 already contains much of the environmental richness that a replacement would have to rebuild: continuous regional geography, hierarchical mountain massifs, species-aware forests and undergrowth, dense species-based grass, carved rivers, lakes/ocean, streamed caves and landmarks, weather, surface wetness/snow, Standard materials, shadows and a complete post-processing chain. Its default High preset enables AO, bloom, god rays and water reflections. These are implementation facts; whether each combination looks good remains a visual judgment. [E01–E07]

The strongest research candidates are **narrow techniques**, not a wholesale V2/V3 renderer or world replacement:

- V3's explicit ridge softening, relief floor, mountain pass and silhouette comparison tools; adapt the shaping ideas within V1's existing terrain authority.
- V2/V3's offline tree LOD/impostor pipeline, especially retaining the V1 species already incorporated into V3. It trades representation rather than simply deleting trees, but alpha overdraw, species proportions and transition artifacts remain important.
- V3's shared, phase-continuous wind infrastructure and directional wind waves on flat water. Actual tree sway is a separate, default-off feature whose recorded GPU gate is not accepted.
- V3's spatial threshold snow field shared by terrain and grass. The author liked ground patches under an override; that is not acceptance of running-clock accumulation, melting or complete winter rendering.
- V2's differentiated bank profiles and flat-water reflection relevance predicate; V3's shadow stabilization and diagnostics; selected character/prop variants rather than replacing V1's entire asset family. [E08–E23]

The largest preservation risk is importing the **V2/V3 material and content policy** along with useful machinery. Their default Lambert conversion, different environmental composition and missing/default-off effects cannot be assumed to preserve V1's grade, contact shading, vegetation depth, water appearance or night atmosphere. V3 is currently adding gated Standard/environment/bloom pilots to address its own plastic-look complaint; those pilots are not evidence that V3's baseline is a visual upgrade. [E19–E21]

There is **no equivalent-workload V1/V2/V3 GPU comparison** in the inspected evidence. Existing numbers belong to particular scenes, builds, devices and effects. No screenshots, animated scenes or GLB contents were rendered/visually inspected during R3. Historical review verdicts and author feedback below are explicitly attributed, not new independent look approval.

## 2. V1 visual preservation baseline

The following are observable mechanisms/assets that can plausibly contribute to the author's preferred V1 identity. Their contribution is an inference from code and recorded feedback, not a new aesthetic verdict.

V1 base terrain uses 64 m chunks, load radius 3 and unload radius 4; High has full LOD scale and filler coverage 0.6. Its regional vegetation batches cover 3×3 chunks, approximately 192 m. V3's continent is 8,192 m wide, with 128 m mesh chunks, desktop terrain radius 1,536 m and tree radius 480 m. These are different presentation contexts, **not equivalent world sizes or exploration workloads**. V1's loaded ring is not its whole world. Distances, terrain height distributions, object scale and camera framing must be compared together. [E02, E03, E07, E08]

Objective preservation checks can cover species/placements, silhouette coverage, heights, color/exposure settings and active passes. Whether the result feels richer, more natural, darker, more realistic or more attractive requires author acceptance.

### Performance evidence, by evidence class

1. **Measured within a documented workload, not portable gains.** V1 result030: seed42 settlement-heavy, High, pixel ratio1, 1920×945, rain, 30 s; 2,311 average draws, 8.00M average triangles, 354 average mirror draws. Its isolated `RENDER` wall time is 23.3 ms baseline, 14.4 ms hiding NPC/fauna, 19.1 ms hiding settlement, 19.6 ms without shadows; no-mirror is 23.4 ms. GPU separation unavailable. These figures justify measuring actors/settlement/shadows in that scene, not concluding reflections are universally free. Result028 had materially different mirror isolation; results are not one transferable budget. [E25]
2. **Measured V2 representation tradeoff.** Nature-pass notes record tight-cut leaf-card LOD improvements after the earlier rectangular-card version regressed march cost despite similar triangles. Round-two high, two alternating Arc140V pairs: dense forest 7.06/7.20→4.56/6.39 ms; march 8.11/13.84→7.20/12.29 ms, with the documented 200 m model ring. WSL instability prevented a third pair. This supports the overdraw technique under that V2 configuration, not V1 GPU savings. [E14]
   V2 also has a later **native Windows laptop** result in PERF's Device results (October 7): Arc140V, seed1337, 1920×1080 at DPR1.1, three 60 s rounds per profile, GPU p95 low/medium/high 8.4/11.8/12.3 ms. The older “no real device measurement” paragraph is stale. This validates that recorded workload, not parity with V1 or long travel; R2 already inventories it.
3. **Measured V3 feature cost with unresolved acceptance.** W4a tree-wind timer study has equal draws/triangles, per-cell median p50 deltas +0.18/+0.58/+0.80/+0.93 ms, median about +0.69 ms; three cells fail +0.5 ms. Integrated Arc140V, three alternating rounds, thermal/power drift, flag verified by URL rather than in-page diagnostics. After matrix-inverse trim, repeat was inconclusive. This is an effect cost, not an optimization. [E18]
4. **Implemented optimizations without a new comparable benchmark here.** V1 region batching, workers for terrain mesh arrays, cached templates/shared materials and selective vegetation LOD; V2/V3 static geometry/material merging, tree bands/impostors, bounded streaming and disposal; V3 far-terrain merge and pooled grass tiles. The October15 audit is a dated inventory, not proof V1 lacks batching or V3 now meets all budgets. [E02,E03,E14,E15,E24]
5. **Theoretical opportunities.** V2's nearby-flat-water predicate may avoid some irrelevant V1 mirror passes; V3's bucketed PMREM may add response at bounded update cost; finer tree LOD may retain density at lower geometry cost. Each needs V1 A/B evidence. Generic KTX2/prewarm/global batching recommendations are not measured wins; historical V1 prewarm failures must not be ignored. [E12,E21,E24]
6. **Quality tradeoffs must be explicit.** Density/submission-prefix reductions, foliage shadow exclusion, shorter rings, lower map/texture resolution and dropping AO/reflections/bloom remove information/effects. They can be acceptable, but a faster frame alone does not certify preservation. V2 SwiftShader reports are useful for CPU preparation, not hardware GPU rankings. [E03,E07,E14,E20,E25]

Texture counts, catalog triangle totals and compressed download bytes are **not VRAM**. No current comparable texture-memory census or long-travel GPU-resource trend was verified. V1 cache/shared-resource ownership and V3 PMREM target disposal are present, but do not prove leak freedom under all rebuild/travel cases. Shader variants/cache keys and snow/wind composition have targeted tests; no current all-variant WebGL run was performed by R3.

### Asset identity, pipeline and provenance findings

- Recursive tree blob comparison: **all 15 `public/assets/animals/*.glb` files are identical between analyzed V2 and V3**. Thus V3 offers no newer bytes for those models. Equality says nothing about V1 equivalence or live animation quality. The common character `anims.glb`, all head variants, hair packs and both equipment packs also match between V2/V3. Four body files differ: `Male_Peasant`, `Male_Peasant_Boots`, `Male_Blacksmith`, `Male_Herbalist`; the other matching body paths inspected have equal blobs. These are product identities, not proof of better rigging. [E22,E23]
- V1 uses GLTFLoader+MeshoptDecoder and SkeletonUtils clone with shared-GPU ownership. V2/V3 additionally build merged templates/packs and simplify assets, frequently retaining only base color and converting Standard to Lambert at runtime. A renderer-side Standard pilot cannot restore normal/roughness maps discarded offline. Asset format compatibility alone does not ensure visual compatibility. [E07,E19,E21,E23]
- The Peasant diagnosis records source cloth/body shoulder separation (about 8 cm Rest, 11 cm Idle), source and kit vertices agreeing, and failed mitigation A/B. Default mitigation is off; the issue remains a Blender/source-family repair, not a verified character improvement. R3 did not reproduce those numerical measurements. [E22]
- Stock `All_Male.blend`/`All_Female.blend` are local-only Quaternius Source pack inputs. There are no committed edited master blends; committed raw GLBs/scripts supply part of the recipe. V3 documentation warns generic village/props rebuild inputs are absent from `_temp/extracted`; do not treat every generated GLB as reproducible on a clean checkout. [E23]
- Quaternius CC0 license files, CC-BY credit files and V1 per-asset credits exist, but local-source and per-product provenance are incomplete. V3 stump/woodfire inventory even says license “n/a”; that needs resolution before reuse. Mixed packs/Poly assets require exact attribution, not a blanket CC0 assumption. No fresh legal/license audit or binary embedded-metadata audit was done. [E23]
- Inventory drift is concrete: V1 MODELS marks rooster as needed although a `public/models/fauna/rooster.glb` exists in the analyzed tree; V3 asset README calls several products unused while newer notes describe wiring/gates. Treat catalog status as a lead: confirm the runtime caller and flag. Total pack triangles, mesh counts or source-source similarity do not establish per-character/per-frame cost. [E23,E24]

## 5. Visual and technical tradeoffs

| Conflict | What the evidence supports | Decision boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Environmental density vs performance | V1 already batches regionally and thins selected mid-detail classes. Offline LOD can retain trees but introduces alpha/transition costs; V2 rectangular cards demonstrated why triangles alone are insufficient. | Decide tolerable canopy/grass coverage loss separately from technical representation. A proposed 10% density cut is a quality tradeoff until matched scenes show otherwise. |
| Realism vs V1 identity | Higher-frequency normals, stronger PBR or environmental reflections can add response; V1's dark/cool grade and stylized species also contribute to identity. | “More realistic” is not an acceptance criterion for replacing its palette, leaf art or night mood. |
| Weather vs budget | Shared uniforms avoid per-instance CPU updates; gust/snow shader ALU and animated depth still cost GPU. W4a has zero geometry delta and measurable added cost. | Choose strength/coverage and hardware budget together; default-off upstream cannot be called free or accepted. |
| Model fidelity vs animation/cost | Extra outfits/faces add variety; merging reduces draws but bind-space, simplification and skin seams can regress. | A good Rest preview is insufficient; compare actual posed source/assembled instance, close and gameplay distance, under V1 light. |
| River/terrain changes vs existing world | V2 bank asymmetry, V3 taper/tarn/breach ideas are separable, but V1 has canonical width/height/carve/shore contracts already. | Hydrology replacement is XL/high exposure; a shader or local profile adaptation may still require ground/water/vegetation agreement. R2 owns broader state consequences. |
| Shadows/reflections/AO vs GPU | V1 has existing map/cadence/content policies. V3 leaf shadow exclusion and reflection-free flat water reduce work by changing the image. | Do not replace effect-rich V1 with a faster effect-poor scene and call it optimization. Look approval must cover contact, canopy shade, movement and night. |
| Rich textures vs startup/resource memory | Packs/atlases reduce loads/material variants in some cases; mixed-source rebuilds and omitted PBR maps constrain fidelity. | Download bytes and RGBA estimates are not measured VRAM. Preserve source, UV transforms, map semantics and cleanup before comparing runtime memory. |

### Handoff

The strongest opportunities are V1-preserving tree representations, targeted V3 mountain shaping, shared wind/flat-water response and patch-based terrain/grass snow.
