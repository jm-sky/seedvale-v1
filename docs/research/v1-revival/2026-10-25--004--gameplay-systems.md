## 1. Executive summary

**V1 already contains substantial integrated item gameplay.** Its strongest preservation assets are actor-reusable inventories; identity-bearing weapons, armor, traps, tents and liquid containers; food batches with storage-rate and species provenance; real pickup/drop/container transfers; working player equipment modifiers; fauna combat; completed-action skill XP; and NPC/household production, provisions, finite merchant stock and artisan orders. General player recipe crafting is a gap, but “V1 has no crafting” would erase its cooking, preservation, construction, repair and NPC-mediated manufacturing.

**V3 offers useful improvements in data organization and presentation.** Its discriminated item definitions, separate per-instance condition, explicit quality versus named rarity/tier, layered armor, loadout comparison, ammunition selection, condition meters, item cards and merchant basket are concrete implementations. Some of the merchant presentation itself derives from V1. Selective reuse can improve V1 without adopting V3's entire inventory or character authority.

**V2 currently supplies the broader integrated reference for general recipe crafting and equipment-aware combat.** Recipe admission, tool/station/skill checks, timed completion, quality, training, merchant purses and household stock are connected to gameplay. Its block/parry, directional dodge and skill-based ranged assistance are real source implementations; they also carry player-only rules, coupling and progression inconsistencies that preclude a blind port.

**V3 is not a finished replacement gameplay stack.** Its world inventory is a different component from the richer inventory sandbox. World eat/drink, needs, gathering, felling, fishing, campfires and crafting exist behind default-off gates. The inspected craft kernel does not apply recipe skill requirements, skill-adjusted duration, training or quality rolls. Combat execution is still unported. SPECIA/skills have pure logic and an inert authoritative character owner, rather than a proven live progression loop.

**The largest risks are semantic:** persistent global item identity versus owner-local IDs; incompatible food models; V1's physical coin item versus a separate purse; six unlayered equipment slots versus seven layered slots; normalized XP-derived skills versus continuous 0–100 proficiency; and replacing concrete NPC resource use with V3's currently abstract home meal/work acts. V3 trade is live but session-only for money and merchant stock, and its current UI/session boundary accepts next accounts rather than revalidating a command.

**Important defects remain on both sides.** V3 review245 independently failed food-row consolidation and fresh-time trade checks; the corresponding source patterns remain at the analyzed SHA. V1 also has freshness-losing purchase branches and NPC food-selection helpers that do not themselves reject spoiled food. These are source-observed concerns, not runtime reproductions by R4. Preserving V1 means preserving its useful semantics and testing its weak boundaries, not declaring every existing loop correct.

## 6. Data and integration risks

### Identifiers, instances and saves

1. **Definitions are not aliases merely because their names look related.** V3 legacy metadata maps e.g. V1 `damascus_knife` to `damascus_dagger`, `masterwork_sword` to `fenmoor_sword`, and `masterwork_hunting_bow` to `marlow_longbow`. V1 `beam`, V3 `log/board`, V1 `iron_rod` and V3 `iron_ingot` have different acquisition/production roles. Inspect values, capabilities and every recipe before proposing mappings.
2. **Quality semantics differ.** V1 armor masterwork is a per-piece quality. V3 masterwork is a rare named definition tier, separate from exceptional quality. Conflation changes stats, availability, pricing and progression.
3. **V1 physical identity survives transfers; V3 transfer assigns a destination-local ID or merges a row.** V1 also uses instance identity in authored item ownership, containers/keys, placements, selected gear and world drops. V3 `legacyIds` does not migrate any of these. A V3 transfer used beneath V1 would require preservation or explicit rebinding of every external reference.
4. **Persistence contracts are fundamentally different.** V1 v56 has a real migration pipeline. V3 v11 parser rejects other versions under its pre-release policy; its serializer is no V1 importer. Existing V1 food batches, liquid/milk content, tent condition, skill XP, armor references and coins cannot be deserialized as V3 ItemInstance by renaming fields.
5. **Global identity also has existing limitations:** V1 generic `createItemInstanceId` uses Date.now plus a process-local counter; V3 uses deterministic inventory counters. This is a determinism/uniqueness tradeoff to investigate, not a reason to silently discard persistent IDs.

### Equipment, progression and authoritative UI

V1 damage uses scalar protection/modifier composition and its timed attack fields. V3 uses type-specific resistance and coverage-weighted loadout summaries; its weapon definition omits V1 wind-up/hit-window/arc. Reusing V3 numbers would change working combat even if the screen still looks correct. Quality/edge must not be applied twice.

V1 XP→0.2…1 and V2/V3 continuous 0…100 skill values have different initial states, award events and book semantics. V1 Defense/Archery/Repair do not map bijectively to V3 Melee/Ranged/Blacksmith/Construction. V3's 13 skills include Mining/Riding beyond V2's eleven, but a definition/training-source row is not a live writer. A scale conversion alone would not preserve earned capabilities or NPC expertise.

V3 low-level presentation reuse is plausible because both versions use Vue/TypeScript/Tailwind. It is **not independent of dependencies**: cards import V3 item guards, performingItem, quality/format helpers and icon registries; EquipmentPanel calls V3 inventory/loadout math; MerchantScreen runs settlement/service algorithms; InventoryScreen emits entire transformed inventories. V1 uses its UI store plus injected app handlers and existing modal/input rules. Reuse a derived view contract and command callbacks; importing the screen unchanged imports gameplay policy.

Specific unsafe assumptions:
- Sandbox `InventoryScreen.drop` calls `removeInstance`; it does not spawn a physical world item.
- Sandbox `repair/sharpen` has no payment/material debit, explicitly for judging values.
- `CharacterDemo` supplies local refs, fixture gear and training buttons; its displayed changes are not live world rewards.
- WorldInventoryScreen explicitly has no equip/use/drop beyond its gated food/water/craft hosts.
- V3 `TradePanel` computes new accounts in UI and `SceneSession.tradeCommit` publishes them without fresh basket/revision/time validation. This is a stale-state risk beyond merely formatting the screen.

### Food and trading correctness

**V1 strengths are real but unevenly applied.** FoodBatch stores acquisition time, accumulated effective age, checkpoint time, decay modifier and optional sourceSpecies; stored food uses 0.5× decay versus carried 1×. `transferInventoryCount` checkpoints batches through both owners. Cooking `inheritProcessedFoodBatch` keeps used lifetime and species. Those semantics would disappear in a simple fixed deadline replacement.

**V1 purchase risk, source-observed:** `settleMerchantStockTransaction` and `settleOwnedGoodsPurchase` remove count goods and `add(kind,count)` without moved batches/time. `settleOwnedGoodsBarterTransaction` likewise uses plain remove/add for incoming purchases, while *offered* goods use `transferInventoryCount(...,nowDays)`. Thus freshness is not preserved uniformly, despite strong generic transfer helpers. Catalog merchant offers are removed and net player coins adjusted rather than deposited into a conserved merchant wallet/stock; ordinary NPC barter has real receiving owners and purse limits. Verify purchase/refund/save routes separately.

**V1 NPC safety risk, source-observed:** `takeOneFoodItem` selects the first catalog food kind with a positive removable count; `consumeOnePersonalFood` delegates to it. Neither helper filters spoilage or applies player food-safety illness logic. The player consume path uses freshness/safety, so a universal “food safety works for all actors” claim is unsupported.

**V2 differences:** stack freshness is remaining calendar hours with merge tolerance; player preservation preserves the weakest remaining fraction, but the NPC drying act does not. Equipment may reside in `Human.eq` apart from inventory; replacing it with references is semantic work, not just typing.

**V3 known failures, checked structurally at the current SHA:** review245 at `56dd979` reported two successful eat/cook paths leaving multiple expired input rows and a trade snapshot accepted after expiry. Current `eatFromKit` still returns takeInstance without consolidation; `completeCraft` consolidates only the added output product; `publishTrade` still stamps nowS only when publishing and only with eating enabled. Trade modal advances world time (`pausesSim:false`), while commit accepts precomputed accounts. R4 did not rerun those reproductions; report245's historical FAIL is not a new R4 execution result. Missing species/poison provenance, variable storage decay and full freshness UI further prevent treating this as V1 parity.

### NPC autonomy, economy and event hooks

A new player inventory cannot replace household stock, temporary carried work, persistent personal belongings, merchant inventory or artisan reserved output. Production may consume both bulk stock and concrete items; location proximity does not grant ownership. V1 `transferAllInventoryContents` is atomic per row, not globally rollback-capable if a capacity-limited destination fails mid-move.

V3 NPC home meals directly fill hunger without concrete food debit, and work completion rotates visits without manufacturing stock. Those are implemented NPC routines, not a substitute for V1's autonomous item economy. V2 supplies useful concrete precedents but its work acts and player recipes diverge (e.g. fletching/materials, drying).

Existing item-acquisition/ownership, combat hits, skill awards and artisan collection connect to downstream completion/progression hooks. Porting these must preserve successful committed-event timing and exact item references. Quest design/dialogue are excluded here; only item/event compatibility is flagged for R5/Astra.

## 8. Open questions for Astra

1. Which concrete V1 item loops are non-negotiable preservation demonstrations: food provenance/storage, artisan orders, finite NPC trade, targeted medicine, camp repair, fauna hunting, or all of these? Author playtest can establish the observable baseline.
2. Is the desired gain primarily clearer item/equipment presentation, more item variety, general player manufacturing, deeper combat choices, or a different progression model? These have markedly different integration costs.
3. Can V3 item cards/meters/preview layouts be adapted to V1's existing catalog/handlers while retaining V1 semantic labels and effective-attribute feedback? This is feasible in principle; it does not require a new inventory authority.
4. Is layered armor/type-specific damage worth changing V1 damage, NPC loadouts, saves and content availability? A second visual slot is not the same as a new mitigation model.
5. How should existing earned Defense/Archery/Repair and XP-based books survive any expanded skill vocabulary? Additional SPECIA attributes need real consumers; no conversion/balance policy is selected here.
6. Should a general player craft interface complement NPC artisan expertise and real stock, and which meaningful outputs justify the added loop? V2 is the fuller mechanical reference; V3 contributes a smaller staged kernel and panel.
7. Does weapon breakage/repair add enough value to justify the changed viability of existing gear and autonomous NPC hunting/defense? Its full lifecycle, not just wear math, would be new in V1.
8. What trade behavior is intended for catalog merchants versus ordinary NPCs: finite purses, barter exceptions, household access and price consequences? V3's session-only trade is not a ready durable economy replacement.
9. Which food semantics must remain: variable storage decay, meat species, raw-food risks, NPC safety and useful spoilage feedback? Review245 must be resolved before V3 expiry code can be relied on as a complete reference.
10. Do parry/dodge/aim assistance justify gameplay changes beyond preserving V1 fauna combat? V2's player-only restrictions, ally lock eligibility and training timing require explicit choices.
11. Which candidate systems need a focused follow-up experiment because static evidence cannot settle runtime accessibility, save safety or actual gameplay benefit? R4 supplies checks, not an order of work.
