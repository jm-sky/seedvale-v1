## 1. Executive summary

The existing Three.js/WebGL2 stack, chunk streaming, worker pool, actor-local skeleton sharing and separated simulation/presentation owners should be **retained provisionally**, not rewritten. Source-backed correctness problems deserve priority over speculative FPS gains. The five reviews identify three recurring integration weaknesses:

1. **Resource and state validity at lifecycle boundaries:** per-actor palette retirement, N8AO disposal, stale grass callbacks, restored-context shadow/mirror content, incompatible morph/bounds contracts, canceled jobs still occupying workers.
2. **Work completion is not work publication:** a limit on starts does not cap asynchronous terrain/grass/actor attachments, first GPU uploads or simultaneous scene changes. Regional instancing can multiply those costs on a single membership change.
3. **Inadequate attribution before optimization:** current p50/p95/p99 monitors are useful, but worker completion, inter-rAF tasks, first-use upload/shader compilation, mirror GPU time and catch-up workloads cannot yet be causally joined.

The strongest **confirmed visual/correctness** evidence is elevated-terrain shadow coverage (R1 projection), animal animated bounds (S3B-1 shipped-asset CPU counterexamples), garment morph rebinding (S3B-3 production assembly probe) and resource/cancellation lifecycle omissions. The highest **unmeasured scale** candidates are resident actor publication, regional instance reconstruction, one-detached-animal retained-registry scans, O(N²) crowd operations and synchronous time-skip replay. No Critical defect or measured contemporary FPS improvement was established.

**Top five corrective priorities (not authorized work):** (1) animal animated bounds plus garment morph consistency; (2) exact lifecycle/cancellation correctness (player/detached actor retirement, grass stale callback, worker physical occupancy); (3) elevation-aware shadow coverage and GPU capability/recovery contracts; (4) retained-NPC scan plus instrumentation of crowd/skip scaling; (5) measured terrain/actor/region publication and first-use spikes. Within each group, isolated fixes may precede profiling only when correctness is already proved.

## 5. Cross-system architecture and root causes

**Pipeline:** the main renderer, scene, shadow budget, water mirror and composer have distinct owners and measured limits, but real GPU work may run across shadow, reflected scene, beauty, AO/SMAA/bloom and first-use uploads. F16-01/02/04 are **validity** problems; F16-05/07/08/11 are potential GPU/visual trade-offs; F16-06/17/31 obstruct performance attribution. A mirror resolution change cannot substitute for measuring the reflected *scene*.

**Streaming:** chunk work is data-only in the existing worker pool; main thread creates Three.js objects. Cancellation ownership, resource identity and GPU first-use belong to different stages. F16-12/13/14/15 can amplify each other during rapid traversal, but a one-start budget cannot prevent N ready callbacks + region reconstruction + first draw in adjacent frames. Physical occupancy correction is a pool invariant, not an argument to move all rendering into workers.

**Actors:** model cache and per-actor skeleton sharing reduce duplicate base assets/palettes **within** an actor; they do not remove per-part draw calls or animation CPU. F16-19/20/24/27 are correctness/lifetime problems, while F16-21/22/23/25 concern cost and presentation policy. Avoid coupling an animation visibility optimization to authoritative NPC needs/combat or using invalid fauna bounds as a culling oracle.

**Simulation and time:** loaded residents and materialized fauna perform live decisions; distant entities retain identities through checkpoint/materialization systems rather than full-world per-frame AI. The retained NPC registry still leaks into the live detached-animal path (F16-28). Crowd pair loops, skip catch-up and fauna bookkeeping are different cost shapes (per frame, event burst, day rollover). A fixed-step overhaul might harm order/stock conservation and is not a safe default.

**Ownership boundaries to preserve/correct:** renderer owns GPU pass resources and validity after context restore; chunk/grass owners validate request+record and publish ready work; worker pool tracks physical occupancy separately from logical cancellation; settlement owner accepts/rolls back actors and must not bind simulation identity to delayed visuals; player/fauna/settlement terminal owners dispose only actor-owned resources; existing perf monitor coordinates provenance. Local improvements are sufficient **as the starting architecture**. A global frame scheduler is neither warranted by present evidence nor a solution to unbounded GPU submissions by itself.

**Conflicting options:** conservative quadruped bounds increase edge draw calls but fix false rejection; stopping offscreen animation can break reappearance; forcing lower vegetation or NPC density weakens approved world richness; an HDR mirror can improve highlights at GPU memory cost; more aggressive prewarm shifts cost to loading and does not remove uploads; fixed-step catch-up can cause a worse spiral. None is automatically selected.
