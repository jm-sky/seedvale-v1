## 1. Executive summary

V1 already has the most important preservation foundation: one integrated quest runtime with authored and world-derived definitions, stable NPC references, nonlinear objectives, report-before-reward semantics, heard-dialogue journal notes, social consequences, deferred location research, discovery state and a working map/minimap. Its local errands coexist with cave investigations, contested property, treasure chronicles, profession quests and actual settlement problems. V1 is not an empty shell awaiting V3's quest portfolio. [E01–E07]

The strongest **candidates for evaluation**, not approved ports, are:

- **V2 stories:** Night Torches (G03), Well and Rumor (G08), The Map That Missed the River (Q05), and selected treasure investigations such as Q11. They contain structured dialogue, choices and executable source definitions. Their physical, social and economic consequences vary in completeness; a written ending is not necessarily a real simulation change. [E09–E11]
- **V3 map annotations and overlap selection:** bounded personal markers, notes on discovered places, explicit hit testing and stale-editor protection. These can enrich V1's existing map without replacing its geography or discovery authority. [E16–E18]
- **V3 conversation contracts and ordinary-life text:** stable line/option identities, supplier checks, stale-command rejection and bounded engagement; selected role-specific text could broaden V1 interactions. The resolver does not supply general reputation, rumour knowledge or NPC intelligence. [E13–E14]
- **Audio mechanisms rather than blanket voicing:** V2/V3 decoded-buffer budgets and V3 exact-text/clip validation. V1 already has integrated spatial voices and contextual barks. V3's 317 tracked MP3 files do not constitute 317 integrated dialogue lines; its three-line exact manifest is unverified and inert. [E08, E15, E19]

The largest risks are losing V1 content through accidental adoption of V3 curation decisions, duplicating V1 quest/knowledge owners, importing quest-owned NPC holds or fabricated world effects, changing travel-time semantics, breaking stable identities/save progress, and replacing existing UI with components that depend on V3 session contracts. V1's dialogue time-control monkey patches and nonpersistent wild quest targets also need explicit preservation/acceptance decisions. [E01–E08, E12–E14]

**No game was played, rendered, listened to or benchmarked for R5. No application tests were executed.** Source wiring and selected test bodies were inspected. Existing reviews and acceptance records are historical evidence only. This report supplies no new end-to-end-verified quest count and no comparative fun, atmosphere, accessibility or performance verdict.

### Map representation and independent reuse

V1 draws coarse terrain/biome cells, fog, labels, known locations, player heading and selected targets; its inspected world/minimap drawers contain no separate road-polyline or river-polyline layer. V2's `worldMapImage.ts` caches a biome/height raster per world and draws its roads. V3's `world/map/mapRaster.ts` builds a cached sea/lake/biome/height/hillshade window and simplified river chains; its marker/structure layers sit above that raster. These are concrete representation differences, not a verdict that one full UI should replace another. A V1 road/river overlay could consume V1's own authoritative geometry through its existing map projection; V3 canonical-world raster bounds and IDs must not be copied. Whether a feature is readable at useful zoom and affordable over V1's streamed extent remains unverified. [E07,E12,E17]

### Travel distinctions that matter

V1's targets and V3's compass are **navigation assistance**. V2's `autopilotToSettlement`/`autopilotToPoint` plus `player.ts` are **automatic physical movement**. The V2 executor sets `sim.timeScale` using road/off-road acceleration settings; copying only its path steering would omit an important player/world-time policy. Threat detection clears the commitment; the currently computed steering intent may still reach the remainder of that tick, so same-tick stop behavior needs testing. Stuck point travel can report arrival within 40 m of its final goal. Neither mechanism is instant teleportation. [E12]

V1 has goods transport and travelling merchants, but that does not establish **diegetic player transport services**. Q11's investment/toll narrative likewise is not a ferry implementation. No ordinary live map-click instant-travel path was established in inspected entry points; debug teleportation is not evidence of gameplay fast travel. Preferred guidance, acceleration, danger and transport policies remain author decisions.

## 9. Open questions for Astra

1. Which V1 journeys, relationships and quiet discoveries define the preservation baseline? Should any V3 retirement/merge be reconsidered because its rationale assumes a different future game?
2. For each selected story, may NPCs solve it without the player, and what information or partial ending remains afterward? Which scripts may briefly hold an NPC, and when must needs/danger override the scene?
3. How much guidance should rumours, quest journals, exact markers and personal map notes provide? Should approximate information become exact only on physical discovery?
4. What should an NPC be allowed to claim about events it did not witness or learn? Is V1's bounded settlement news sufficient for the intended stories, or is a specific additional information need demonstrated?
5. Does automatic physical travel fit V1? If so, what are the danger/manual-input/mount/arrival policies, and may it accelerate the world? Separately, do passenger transport or instant travel belong at all?
6. What is the desired voice coverage: atmospheric barks, short player exchanges, selected story scenes or broader dialogue? How should English recordings relate to V1's Polish authored text and cast?

8. Which narrative themes deserve expansion: village misunderstandings, family/property disputes, quiet investigation, hazardous travel or larger treasure arcs? Are Q05/Q11's required real sites and consequences worth their dependency cost?
9. Which existing UI presentation should remain recognizable, and which individual affordances need refresh? What desktop accessibility and input acceptance is required beyond the current baseline?
