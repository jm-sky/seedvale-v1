# V2 Q10–Q13 Curation and Exploration Rewards Direction

**Created at:** 2026-10-14

**Status:** Accepted author narrative direction, detailed implementation and balance deferred.

## D-QUEST-45 — Author decision

- **Q10 What the Mountain Owes — retire as a separate authored quest; merge only compatible ideas into the V1 Abandoned Gold Mine → Mining Colony line.** The V1 line is authoritative for the intended discovery, expedition, persistent mining colony, finite deposits and autonomous gold economy. Q10 must not create a second mine incident, quest economy, overlapping mine-ownership rewards or independent outpost mechanism. Its Agnes/Silas/Baldwin details are reference suggestions only, not newly accepted cast or required beats. Before implementation reconcile the V1 roadmap and plan with current canonical architecture and actual code.
- **Q11 The Bell in Blackwater — keep/rewrite, lower priority.** Retain the marsh/chapel discovery, recovered community property and player-influenced uses of the find. Seasonal water, causeway, boats, unique-animal details and rewards are not approved technical requirements.
- **Q12 The Iron Under the Pine — keep/rewrite.** Retain the special sword, its history and meaningful alternatives for its destination. **Generous, exceptional rewards are welcome** when the accomplishment and economy justify them; do not apply an automatic nerf merely because the equipment is valuable. Exact amounts/items and prerequisites remain open.
- **Q13 The Ash House Vault — keep/rewrite.** Retain the family estate, buried assets, unpaid historical wages/ownership evidence and multiple outcomes. Rebuilding or operating a waystation is not mandatory scope without general systems.

## Author preference — accessible rewarding exploration

The author explicitly welcomes **interesting, simple opportunities with worthwhile payoffs** so the player can earn money and improve equipment relatively early. Suitable world content includes buried treasure near landmarks, valuable objects in caves, discoverable caches and quest rewards. Such discoveries should be meaningfully rewarding, not systematically nerfed into trivial loot; respect rarity, ownership, persistence and the larger economy. This is a positive design direction, **not** an approval of specific treasure counts, drop rates, prices, procedural placements or new loot/cave mechanics.

## Explicit feature approval boundary

**Do not create substantial quest-specific gameplay mechanisms without the author's explicit approval.** Reuse shared world, resources, ownership, NPC autonomy, construction, travel and economy systems first. If a quest needs a substantial missing capability, present the need and a smaller alternative (simplify, defer, or build a shared systemic feature) for author approval before implementing it. A narrative KEEP does not itself authorize the mechanism.

## Deferred

M7 and the relevant system milestones will choose concrete scope, branches, quest availability, world geography, NPC binding, balancing, economic payouts and verification. Distinct treasure quests should not be merged solely because they use ruins, bears, caves, caches or valuable equipment; reconcile genuine shared incidents/locations to avoid duplication.
